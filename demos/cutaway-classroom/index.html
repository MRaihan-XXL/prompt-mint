Why a cutaway

You cannot see breathing from the outside.

A fish gulps water, but the real work happens in a chamber you never see. Cutting the model in half lets the flow, the filaments and the exit valve show up in one frame.

One-way flow

Water enters the mouth and leaves behind the operculum. It never reverses, so fresh oxygen is always arriving. / Air mengalir satu arah.

Thin filaments

Each of the four arches carries nine red filaments, thin enough for oxygen to cross into the blood. / Filamen insang sangat tipis.

Timing matters

Mouth opens, flow slows over the gills, gas swaps, the operculum opens. Five beats, three seconds each. / Lima langkah, tiga detik.

The 5 steps

Water in, oxygen across, water out.

Lesson brief

A 12-minute classroom demo on gas exchange. Project it, then let students drive.

Objectives

  • Trace water from mouth to operculum and name each structure.
  • Explain why filaments are thin and numerous.
  • Compare a gill with a lung: same job, different path.

Run it

  • Press Start lesson; the five steps auto-advance every 3 seconds.
  • Pause with Space and click a numbered callout to zoom in.
  • Swap to Specimen B and repeat.

Ask the class

  • What would happen if the operculum stayed shut?
  • Why is the blood red where the water turns red?
